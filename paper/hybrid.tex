\section{A hybrid approach}
\label{sec:hybrid}

As discussed in \cref{sec:introduction}, a larger security margin and the removal of
algebraic structure address different hypothetical classes of attack, representing different design principles. At this time, it is unclear which
of the two stances on conservatism should be prioritized. A hybrid KEM does not require choosing between them. Hybrid KEMs run two KEMs side by side and
derive a single shared key from both shared secrets and both ciphertexts; with an appropriate combiner, the result is IND-CCA secure as long as at
least one component is~\cite{PKC:GiaHeuPoe18,PQCRYPTO:BBFGS19}.

We consider a hybrid of \kaiburr-1792 and FrodoKEM-976 and compare it with FrodoKEM-1344, the most conservative standardized FrodoKEM parameter set. We
have not implemented this hybrid as a single KEM. Its sizes in \cref{tab:hybrid} are exact, since they are the sums of the component sizes. Its cycle
counts in \cref{tab:hybrid-cycles} are the sums of the measured component medians and do not
include the combiner, which hashes both shared secrets and both ciphertexts.

The hybrid is smaller than FrodoKEM-1344, by 14.7\% in public-key size and 13.0\% in ciphertext size, and the sum of its component cycle counts is about
1.7$\times$ lower. Almost all of this speedup comes from FrodoKEM-976 itself, and \kaiburr-1792 accounts for about 2\% of the hybrid's cycles.

A hybrid KEM is as hard to break as the stronger component. Against improvements to lattice attacks in general, this is \kaiburr-1792: its
cheapest known lattice attack costs $2^{467.3}$ compared with $2^{281.7}$ for FrodoKEM-1344. Against an attack that exploits the module structure of
\kaiburr but does not apply to plain LWE, the hybrid retains a structural hedge, but at the level of FrodoKEM-976 ($2^{225.2}$) rather than
FrodoKEM-1344 ($2^{281.7}$). The hybrid is therefore preferable to FrodoKEM-1344 when this level of protection against structural attacks is
considered sufficient. A deployment that wants no reduction in structural margin can instead add \kaiburr-1792 to FrodoKEM-1344; by the same component
measurements, this costs about 1\% more cycles before the combiner and 13--14\%
more bandwidth.
