\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\paragraph{Notation.} Throughout the paper, we use \textsc{Kyber} and ML-KEM
interchangeably, except when separating the NIST submission from the standardized version.
We write vectors in bold lowercase and matrices in bold uppercase, $\mathbf{x}^T$ for the transpose of $\mathbf{x}$,
and $\lceil x \rfloor$ for rounding $x$ to the nearest integer.

\paragraph{Ring and module setup.} Let $n=256$ and $q=3329$, and let $R_q=\mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^n+1)$ be \textsc{Kyber}'s
base ring, with coefficients centered in $(-q/2,q/2]$. The modulus satisfies $n \mid (q-1)$, needed for the
number-theoretic transform (NTT) that \textsc{Kyber} uses for polynomial multiplication in $R_q$. A module rank $k$ determines the dimensions of the public matrix
$\mathbf{A}\in R_q^{k\times k}$ and of the secret and error vectors in $R_q^k$. $k=2,3,4$ give ML-KEM-512, -768, and -1024,
respectively~\cite{fips203}.

\paragraph{Centered binomial noise.}
\textsc{Kyber} samples secret and error coefficients from the centered binomial distribution $\mathrm{CBD}_\eta$. It draws $2\eta$ uniform bits
$a_1,\dots,a_\eta,b_1,\dots,b_\eta$ and outputs $\sum_{i=1}^\eta a_i - \sum_{i=1}^\eta b_i$, a value in
$\{-\eta,\ldots,\eta\}$ with variance $\eta/2$. ML-KEM uses two parameters: $\eta_1$ for the secret and error vectors
$\mathbf{s},\mathbf{e}$ in key generation and the randomness $\mathbf{r}$ in encryption, and $\eta_2$ for the encryption errors
$\mathbf{e}_1, e_2$ (see below). ML-KEM-768 and -1024 use $\eta_1=\eta_2=2$; ML-KEM-512 uses $\eta_1=3$ and $\eta_2=2$.
$\mathrm{CBD}_\eta$ is especially desirable because it can be sampled from raw PRF output in constant time without rejection
and uses only bit-counting and a subtraction.

\paragraph{Module-LWE.} Let $\chi$ be a distribution over $R_q$ with small coefficients. The decisional Module Learning With Errors (MLWE)
problem~\cite{DCC:LanSte15} over $R_q$ asks an adversary to distinguish
$(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e})$, where $\mathbf{A}\leftarrow R_q^{m\times k}$ is uniform and $\mathbf{s}\leftarrow \chi^k$,
$\mathbf{e}\leftarrow \chi^m$, from a uniformly random pair in $R_q^{m\times k}\times R_q^m$.
In ML-KEM, $\chi$ samples each coefficient from $\mathrm{CBD}_\eta$; \textsc{Kaiburr} replaces it with the noise
family of Section~\ref{sec:distribution}. Increasing $k$ increases the dimension $kn$
of the underlying lattice problem and is the main lever \textsc{Kyber} uses to raise its claimed security level~\cite{kyberround1}.

\paragraph{The \textsc{Kyber} public-key encryption scheme.}
Key generation expands a public $32$-byte seed $\rho$ into $\mathbf{A}\in R_q^{k\times k}$, directly in the NTT domain, by
rejection sampling where SHAKE-128 output is parsed into $12$-bit candidates, and those smaller than $q$ are accepted as coefficients.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Key generation:} sample $\mathbf{s},\mathbf{e}\leftarrow\chi^k$ and set $\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}$.
    The public key is $(\mathbf{t},\rho)$ and the secret key is $\mathbf{s}$.
  \item \emph{Encryption} of a message $m\in\{0,1\}^{n}$, viewed as a polynomial with binary coefficients: sample
    $\mathbf{r}\leftarrow\chi^k$, $\mathbf{e}_1\leftarrow\chi^k$, and $e_2\leftarrow\chi$, and compute
    $\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_1$ and $v=\mathbf{t}^T\mathbf{r}+e_2+\lceil q/2\rfloor\cdot m$.
  \item \emph{Decryption:} compute $v-\mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{u}$ and decode each coefficient to $1$ if it is closer to
    $\lceil q/2\rfloor$ than to $0$, and to $0$ otherwise.
\end{itemize}
The IND-CPA security of this scheme reduces to MLWE with $\chi$. The public key is an instance with $m=k$, and,
with $\mathbf{t}$ replaced by a uniform vector, the ciphertext is an instance with $m=k+1$.
Expanding the decryption step gives $v-\mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{u}=\lceil q/2\rfloor\cdot m+\tilde{n}$ with noise term
$$\tilde{n} = \mathbf{e}^T\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{e}_1 + e_2 .$$

\paragraph{Ciphertext compression.}
Instead of transmitting $\mathbf{u}$ and $v$ at full $12$-bit precision, ML-KEM compresses them by rounding each coefficient
to $d_u$ and $d_v$ bits, respectively, with $(d_u,d_v)=(10,4)$ for ML-KEM-512 and -768 and $(11,5)$ for ML-KEM-1024.
This reduces the ciphertext from $384(k+1)$ to $32(d_u k+d_v)$ bytes, e.g.\ from $1536$ to $1088$ bytes for ML-KEM-768. The receiver
decompresses to $\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{c}_u$ and $v+c_v$, where $\mathbf{c}_u$ and $c_v$ are the rounding errors, so the noise term becomes
$$\tilde{n} = \mathbf{e}^T\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{e}_1 - \mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{c}_u + e_2 + c_v ;$$
this trades a higher decryption-failure probability for a smaller ciphertext. The public key is never compressed and has
$384k+32$ bytes. Throughout the paper, ``ciphertext compression'' refers to this rounding of $\mathbf{u}$ and $v$.

\paragraph{From PKE to KEM.} \textsc{Kyber}'s IND-CPA-secure public-key encryption scheme is lifted to an IND-CCA2-secure KEM using a
variant of the Fujisaki--Okamoto (FO) transform with implicit rejection~\cite{fips203}. Decapsulation decrypts, then re-encrypts the
recovered message with the derived randomness, and returns a pseudorandom key if the result differs from the received ciphertext.
We leave the FO transform unchanged for our construction of \textsc{Kaiburr}.

\paragraph{Decryption failure.} Decryption is correct as long as every coefficient of $\tilde{n}$ is smaller than $q/4$ in absolute value.
We refer to the decryption-failure probability $\delta$ as the probability, over the randomness of key generation and
encryption, that at least one of the $n$ coefficients of $\tilde{n}$ exceeds this threshold.
\footnote{Even though a single decryption error may not sound catastrophic, it is well established that once an attacker finds a single failure,
they can use it as a compass to drastically accelerate finding more failures, a type of attack known as ``directional failure-boosting''~\cite{EC:DAnRosVir20}.}
To compute $\delta$, we adapt the \textsc{Kyber} team's scripts~\cite{kyber-scripts}. Each coefficient of
$\mathbf{e}^T\mathbf{r}$ and of $\mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{e}_1$ is a signed sum of $kn$ products of two independent noise coefficients.
The scripts compute the distribution of a single coefficient of $\tilde{n}$ by convolution, take its tail mass beyond $q/4$,
and multiply by $n$ (a union bound over the coefficients). With compression, the rounding errors $\mathbf{c}_u$ and $c_v$ are additionally
modeled as independent of the remaining terms.

\paragraph{Core-SVP hardness estimates.} We estimate MLWE hardness following the standard Core-SVP methodology~\cite{USENIX:ADPS16}
used throughout the \textsc{Kyber} submission documents and implemented in the \textsc{Kyber} team's public estimator
scripts~\cite{kyber-scripts}. An adversary is modeled as
running BKZ lattice reduction with block size $b$ in either the primal or the dual lattice attack. The cost of BKZ-$b$ is approximated by a single call to an
SVP oracle in dimension $b$, whose cost is estimated as $2^{cb}$ operations, with $c\approx 0.292$ for classical sieving~\cite{SODA:BDGL16} and
$c\approx 0.265$ for quantum sieving~\cite{laarhoven-thesis}. For a given parameter set, the scripts search over block size
$b$ and number of samples $m$ for the cheapest successful attack, and report its $\log_2$ cost as the
\emph{classical} and \emph{quantum} Core-SVP security level. The scripts depend on the noise distribution only through its standard deviation.
We report both levels throughout. For reference, the same scripts give classical/quantum Core-SVP levels of $117/106$, $181/164$, and $253/230$ for 
ML-KEM-512, -768, and -1024, taking the cheaper of the primal and dual attacks as for our parameter sets.

\paragraph{Lattice estimator.} We cross-check the Core-SVP numbers with the lattice estimator of Albrecht et al.~\cite{estimator},\footnote{\url{https://github.com/malb/lattice-estimator},
commit \texttt{53da598}.}
which evaluates a wider range of attacks than the \textsc{Kyber} scripts like the primal uSVP and BDD attacks, the primal hybrid attack, the dual attack,
the dual-hybrid attack of~\cite{matzov2022lwe}, coded BKW, and the Arora--Ge attack with Gr\"obner bases. Its full estimate costs lattice reduction
with the MATZOV model~\cite{matzov2022lwe} by default, rather than with Core-SVP. The estimator also provides a \texttt{rough} mode that only considers
the usually cheapest attacks, uSVP and dual hybrid, and costs them with the Core-SVP model $2^{0.292b}$, which makes it closer to the \textsc{Kyber}
scripts. We model each parameter set as an LWE instance of dimension $256k$ with $256k$ samples, $q=3329$, and secret and error coefficients
of standard deviation $\sigma(t)$ (Section~\ref{sec:distribution}).
