\section{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

We benchmark on three Intel generations, each single-core
with Hyper-Threading and Turbo Boost disabled. We report
the benchmarks pinned to both the performance and efficient cores separately. Our code is compiled
with gcc-version.

All three KEMs are measured by a single benchmark driver, adapted from the
one used in~\cite{fvk}. After $1000$ untimed warm-up iterations,
we take the median of $10001$ consecutive measurements together with its
interquartile range ($Q1$ - $Q3$). This is then repeated three times and we report the median
run with its own quartiles. Every iteration checks that the decapsulated
shared secret matches the encapsulated one.

Randomness comes from Linux \texttt{getrandom}. FrodoKEM and \textsc{Kaiburr}
call it within \texttt{keypair} and \texttt{enc}, so that syscall is inside
the measured region. HQC instead seeds a SHAKE-256 PRNG once, which we seed
from \texttt{getrandom} before timing begins. \textsc{Kaiburr}'s
derandomized entry points, reported separately, isolate the scheme cost
from randomness generation.
