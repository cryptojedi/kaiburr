\section{Formally-verified bounds for \kaiburr}
\label{sec:verification}

In this section, we show that removing compression turns the decryption-failure probability from a heuristic estimate into a
formally verified bound.\footnote{Removing compression also removes a step that is costly to mask in ML-KEM decapsulation, which
existing work avoids with a comparison technique~\cite{TCHES:BGRSV21}. We do not study masking for \kaiburr further in this paper.}

\paragraph{Why compression is the obstruction.}
In the noise expression for ML-KEM,
$\tilde{n} = \mathbf{e}^T\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{e}_1 - \mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{c}_u + e_2 + c_v$ (Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}),
the rounding errors $\mathbf{c}_u$ and $c_v$ are the only source of difficulty~\cite{BarbosaKLSS25}. Because the correctness adversary
holds the secret key, no direct MLWE reduction is available, which forces a worst-case elimination of $c_v$ and a split into two
sub-bounds. For ML-KEM-768, the fully provable bound is therefore $2^{-80}$, while the heuristic bound that just assumes
rounding-of-uniform is $2^{-164}$, a gap of roughly 84 bits~\cite{BarbosaKLSS25}. FrodoKEM, which admits no ciphertext compression,
has no such gap.

\paragraph{What we prove.}
We adapt the EasyCrypt framework of~\cite{BarbosaKLSS25}, including its theory of distributions over the ring and its OCaml
implementation for computing a sound upper bound on the final tail probability, to \textsc{Kaiburr}. For each parameter set, we
prove that for every adversary that receives both the public and the secret key and chooses the message, decryption fails with
probability at most $\delta$, the bound in Table~\ref{tab:bounds}. This is the notion of correctness used in the Fujisaki--Okamoto
bound. Since the rounding terms vanish, the noise collapses to
$\tilde{n} = \mathbf{e}^T\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{s}^T\mathbf{e}_1 + e_2$, which does not depend on the message chosen by the adversary.
Since $f(t)$ is non-trivial and symmetric around 0, every coefficient is then identically distributed, and a union bound reduces the
failure probability to $256$ times the tail of a single coefficient, with no additional MLWE term. As in~\cite{BarbosaKLSS25}, the
proof is in the random-oracle model, and the tail is computed outside EasyCrypt by OCaml code that mirrors the EasyCrypt
expression and rounds upward, which is the only step not checked in EasyCrypt.
The same framework with the original $\mathrm{CBD}_2$ gives the machine-checked bounds for the warmup in Table~\ref{tab:cbd-baseline}.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:bounds} shows that our provable bound closely tracks the \textsc{Kyber} team's own estimate for every parameter set,
so for \textsc{Kaiburr} there is no gap between a heuristic and a provable bound to close. The price \textsc{Kaiburr} pays for this
is ciphertext size, which remains within our fixed communication budget.
